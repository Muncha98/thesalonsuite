Acrylic Nail Monomer to Powder Ratio Guide

Incorrect monomer-to-powder ratios cause either brittle service breakdown (too dry) or contact dermatitis and lifting (too wet). Master bead consistency with this ratio guide.

Professional Safety Notice

  • Caution: Ensure adequate salon ventilation — acrylic monomer fumes can cause respiratory irritation with prolonged exposure in poorly ventilated spaces.
  • Caution: Avoid product contact with skin around the cuticle — this increases risk of lifting, irritation, and fungal infection under the enhancement.

Why Acrylic Bead Consistency Changes by Nail Zone

Acrylic application uses three distinct zones on the nail, each needing a different liquid-to-powder (monomer-to-polymer) ratio: a drier bead near the cuticle to avoid flooding the proximal fold, a medium structural bead at the apex for strength, and a wetter bead at the free edge for smooth sculpting and extension.

Using the same wet bead everywhere risks flooding the cuticle area (a common cause of lifting and infection risk), while using a dry bead everywhere makes smooth sculpting at the free edge difficult.

💅 How to use this guide

  1. Select the nail zone you are currently working on — cuticle zone, apex zone, or free edge zone.
  2. Pick up the matching liquid-to-powder ratio bead.
  3. Check that the bead surface satin-smooths within about 3 seconds of powder pickup — too wet or dry a bead takes longer or behaves unpredictably.
Example: the apex (structural) zone uses roughly a 1.5:1 liquid-to-powder ratio for a medium bead that builds strength without excess product.

Rules to Remember

  • 1. Never flood the proximal fold: A wet bead near the cuticle is one of the most common causes of lifting and product touching skin — always use the driest bead in this zone.
  • 2. Bead consistency should satin-smooth within seconds: If the bead takes much longer than 3 seconds to smooth after powder pickup, the ratio is off for that zone — adjust liquid or powder dip accordingly.
