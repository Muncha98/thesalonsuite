Professional Safety Notice

  • Caution: Always perform a 48-hour client skin allergy patch test before brow lamination services.
  • Caution: Never apply lamination solution to brows that are already chemically compromised (recently tinted, waxed with irritation, or over-processed) without allowing recovery time first.

Why Brow Lamination Timing Depends on Hair Texture

Brow lamination works by breaking and reforming the hair's internal bonds to reshape it — Step 1 (lift) softens and repositions the hair, and Step 2 (set/neutralize) locks the new shape in. Fine, thin brow hair processes faster than coarse, stubborn hair, and using a one-size-fits-all timer risks under- or over-processing.

Matching processing time to hair texture gives a consistent, long-lasting lift without compromising hair integrity.

⏲️ How to use this guide

  1. Assess the client's brow hair texture — fine/thin, medium/normal, or thick/coarse.
  2. Apply Step 1 (lift) for the matching time window, checking fine hair early.
  3. Neutralize with Step 2 for an equal time to Step 1.
  4. Finish with a nourishing oil after the full process.
Example: medium/normal brow hair uses a standard 6–7 minute window for both Step 1 and Step 2.

Rules to Remember

  • 1. Check fine hair early: Fine or thin brow hair processes fastest and can over-lift if left for the full standard window — check at 3 minutes rather than waiting for the timer.
  • 2. Never exceed brand maximum, even for coarse hair: Coarse, stubborn hair may need the full window, but exceeding your product's stated maximum risks hair damage regardless of texture.
