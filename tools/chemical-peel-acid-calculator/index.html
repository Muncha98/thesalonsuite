Professional Safety Notice

  • Caution: Always perform a patch test before a full chemical peel service, especially with new clients or unfamiliar products.
  • Caution: Deeper or more aggressive peels require appropriate training and certification — this calculator estimates strength, it does not certify a technician to perform a given peel depth.
  • Caution: Clients with active skin conditions, recent sun exposure, or certain medications (e.g. isotretinoin) may need to be excluded from chemical peel services — always screen thoroughly.

Why Free Acid Percentage Matters More Than Nominal Concentration

A peel labeled "30% acid" does not tell the full story — the actual skin-active strength (free acid) depends heavily on the solution's pH. A lower pH means more of the acid is in its active, unbuffered form and able to penetrate skin, while a higher pH means more of it is neutralized/buffered and less bioavailable.

This calculator uses the Henderson–Hasselbalch relationship with a typical AHA pKa (~3.5) to estimate free acid percentage from nominal concentration and pH — giving a more realistic sense of a peel's actual strength category.

🧪 How to use this calculator

  1. Enter the peel's nominal acid concentration (as labeled on the product).
  2. Enter the solution's pH level.
  3. The calculator estimates free acid percentage and categorizes the peel's practical strength.
Example: a nominal 30% acid solution at pH 2.5 has an estimated free acid of about 27.3%, placing it in the "Active / Professional" strength category.

Rules to Remember

  • 1. This is an estimate, not a replacement for product data: Different acid types have different pKa values — this calculator uses a typical AHA approximation, so always cross-reference your specific product's documented strength and manufacturer protocol.
  • 2. Lower pH always means higher bioavailability at the same nominal strength: Two peels labeled the same nominal percentage can have very different real-world strength if their pH differs — pH is not a minor detail on the label.
