Chemical Safety & Manufacturer Dependency Notice

  • Caution: Only blend developer formulations produced by the same manufacturer and product line.
  • Caution: Never dilute developer with tap water — doing so destabilizes hydrogen peroxide molecules, dilutes buffering agents, and causes uneven lift.
  • Caution: Mathematical ratios calculate volumetric peroxide concentration. Always follow manufacturer processing times and perform required client patch tests.
⚠️ Manufacturer Specification Rule: Stabilizers, lipid carriers, and viscosity thickeners differ between brands. Blending different brands may cause chemical destabilization or uneven tonal results. Mathematical calculations provide proportions, but manufacturer guidelines must always take precedence.

The Chemistry of Developer Volume Blending (The Alligation Method)

Behind the chair, hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) developers are the chemical oxidative drivers of permanent hair color, high-lift blonding, and toner deposit. The "Volume" rating denotes the volume of pure oxygen gas liberated from one volume of liquid developer during complete oxidation (for example, 20 Volume developer releases 20 times its volume in oxygen, representing approximately 6% hydrogen peroxide concentration by weight).

When a colorist runs out of 20 Volume mid-service, canceling appointments or estimating measurements compromises both lift and tonal deposit. Using the pharmaceutical Alligation Alternate method (the rule of mixtures), any two known developer concentrations from the same product line can be mathematically proportioned to create an exact intermediate strength without chemical guesswork.

📐 The Exact Alligation Formula Walkthrough

  1. Step 1: Parts of Low Volume Needed = High Volume − Target Volume
  2. Step 2: Parts of High Volume Needed = Target Volume − Low Volume
  3. Step 3: Total Parts = Parts Low + Parts High
Worked Example: Formulate 60ml of 20 Vol using 10 Vol and 30 Vol:
• Parts of 10 Vol = 30 − 20 = 10 parts
• Parts of 30 Vol = 20 − 10 = 10 parts
• Total Parts = 20 parts → Ratio is 10:10 (1:1 equal parts) → 30ml of 10 Vol + 30ml of 30 Vol = 60ml of 20 Vol.

Critical Station Rules for Mixing Developers

  • 1. Stay Within the Same Brand & Product Line: Never cross-mix developers from different manufacturers (e.g. Wella with Redken). Manufacturers formulate with distinct lipid carriers, viscosity thickeners, and proprietary buffering agents that can destabilize when combined.
  • 2. Measure by Digital Weight (Grams or Ounces): Measuring by eyeball or cup lines introduces 10%–20% volumetric error. Always tare your mixing bowl on a 0.1g digital scale for exact formulation repeatability.
  • 3. Never Dilute Developer with Tap Water: Adding tap water dilutes the preservative system, alters the pH balance, and causes emulsion thinning, leading to packet bleed and patchy lift. Always blend with a lower volume developer.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you mix 10 and 30 volume developer to make 20 volume?

To formulate 20 Volume (6%) developer, mix equal parts (1:1 ratio) of 10 Volume (3%) and 30 Volume (9%) developer. For example, 30ml of 10 Vol + 30ml of 30 Vol equals 60ml of 20 Vol developer.

Can I mix different developer brands together?

No. Different manufacturers formulate developers with proprietary buffers, conditioning agents, and viscosity stabilizers. Blending different brands can cause emulsion breakdown or unpredictable lift.

Can you dilute 40 volume developer with water to make 20 volume?

Never use water to dilute developer. Water dilutes the preservative system, alters the pH balance, and thins the product consistency, causing bleeding into foils or scalp irritation.

How does the Alligation calculation formula work?

The formula uses Pearson's Square (Alligation Alternate): Parts Low = High Vol - Target Vol; Parts High = Target Vol - Low Vol. Total parts equal Parts Low + Parts High.
