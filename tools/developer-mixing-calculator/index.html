The Chemistry of Developer Volume Blending (The Alligation Method)

Behind the chair, hydrogen peroxide ($H_2O_2$) developers are the lifeblood of oxidative hair color, lighteners, and toners. Volume represents the volume of pure oxygen gas released from one volume of liquid developer during oxidation (e.g., 20 Volume developer releases 20 times its volume in oxygen, translating to approximately 6% hydrogen peroxide concentration).

When a colorist runs out of 20 Volume mid-day, canceling appointments or guessing measurements compromises the lift and deposit. Using the pharmaceutical Alligation Alternate method (the rule of mixtures), any two known developer concentrations can be mathematically proportioned to create an exact intermediate strength.

The Exact Alligation Formula

Step 1: Parts of Low Volume Needed = High Volume - Target Volume
Step 2: Parts of High Volume Needed = Target Volume - Low Volume
Step 3: Total Parts = Parts Low + Parts High
Example: To make 60ml of 20 Vol using 10 Vol and 30 Vol:
• Parts of 10 Vol = 30 - 20 = 10 parts
• Parts of 30 Vol = 20 - 10 = 10 parts
• Total Parts = 20 parts ➔ Ratio is 10:10 (1:1 equal parts) ➔ 30ml of 10 Vol + 30ml of 30 Vol = 60ml of 20 Vol.

3 Critical Station Rules for Mixing Developers

  • Stay Within the Same Brand & Line: Never cross-mix developers from different manufacturers (e.g., Redken with Wella). Different brands use proprietary buffering agents, lipid stabilizers, and viscosity thickeners that react unpredictably when blended.
  • Always Measure by Digital Weight (Grams): Measuring by eyeball or cup lines introduces 10%–20% volumetric variance. Use a 0.1g digital scale for repeatable tone consistency.
  • Never Dilute Developer with Tap Water: Adding tap water dilutes the preservative system, destabilizes peroxide molecules, and thins the viscosity, causing lightener to bleed into adjacent foil packets. Always blend with 10 Vol or clear developer.
