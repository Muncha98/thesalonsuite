Professional Safety Notice

  • Caution: Always patch test a new essential oil blend before full application.
  • Caution: Some essential oils are not safe for use during pregnancy, on children, or on certain skin conditions — screen clients before use.
  • Caution: Never apply undiluted ("neat") essential oil directly to skin unless specifically directed by the oil’s documented safety data.
⚠️ Manufacturer Specification Rule: Always cross-reference the specific essential oil’s manufacturer or supplier safety data sheet for its recommended maximum dilution, as this varies by oil. Mathematical calculations provide proportions, but manufacturer guidelines must always take precedence.

Essential Oil Dilution: Getting the Drop Count Right

Essential oils are highly concentrated and must be diluted in a carrier oil before topical application — too strong a dilution risks skin sensitization or irritation, while too weak reduces the intended therapeutic or aromatic effect.

This calculator converts a carrier oil volume and a target dilution percentage into an exact essential oil drop count, using standard drop-to-volume conversions.

💧 How to use this calculator

  1. Enter the amount of carrier oil you are using.
  2. Select your target dilution percentage based on the intended use (see reference table).
  3. Add the exact number of essential oil drops shown.
Example: 30ml of carrier oil at a 2% dilution (standard full body massage) needs 12 drops of essential oil.

Common Dilution Targets

Dilution % Typical Use
0.5% Sensitive skin, facial, or children
1.0% Daily facial use / sensitive skin
2.0% Standard full body massage
3.0% Localized muscle relief
5.0% Acute, short-term application only

Rules to Remember

  • 1. Higher is not always better: Exceeding recommended dilution percentages increases the risk of skin sensitization — more concentrated is not automatically more effective.
  • 2. Different essential oils have different safe maximums: Some oils (e.g. cinnamon, oregano) require lower maximum dilutions than others — always check the specific oil’s safety data before formulating.
