Mathematical calculations provide proportions, but manufacturer guidelines must always take precedence.

Why Grey Hair Needs a Base-to-Fashion Blend

Grey hair has little to no natural background pigment — it is translucent, not simply "faded" like regrown natural hair. A fashion or demi-permanent shade applied alone on resistant grey will often look sheer, cool, or ashy because there is no underlying warmth for it to deposit onto.

A dedicated Natural/Base (N) series restores that missing background pigment, giving the fashion shade something to sit on top of. The more grey present, the more base is needed relative to the fashion shade.

👵 How to use this calculator

  1. Estimate the client's grey percentage by sectioning the hair and visually assessing coverage.
  2. Enter your total color batch size (the full amount you plan to mix).
  3. Use the resulting Base (N) and Fashion Shade gram split to build your formula in the bowl.
  4. Process with 20 Volume developer at a 1:1 or 1:1.5 ratio for a full 45 minutes on resistant cuticles.
Example: 50% grey hair with a 60g total batch needs 30g Natural Base (N) + 30g Fashion Shade.

Rules to Remember

  • 1. More grey means more base, not more developer: Resistant grey needs more Natural Base pigment in the mix, not a higher developer volume — overprocessing with excess peroxide does not improve coverage and risks damage.
  • 2. Full processing time matters on resistant cuticles: Grey hair's compact cuticle structure resists pigment deposit — cutting the 45-minute process short is the most common cause of grey "bleed-through" after a few washes.
