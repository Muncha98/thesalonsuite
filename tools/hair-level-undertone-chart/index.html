Understanding the Color Wheel Rule

Every time hair is lightened, it passes through a predictable sequence of underlying pigments — warm red at the darkest levels, through orange, into yellow and pale yellow at the lightest levels. This is the "exposed pigment" you are working against whenever you tone.

To neutralize an unwanted warm tone, you apply the complementary (opposite) color from the color wheel. The chart below maps each of the ten standard levels of lift to its exposed pigment and the complementary toner family that cancels it out.

🎨 How to read the chart

  1. Identify the level of lift you have reached (or are aiming for) after lightening — Level 1 is black, Level 10 is the lightest pale yellow/white.
  2. Find that level's exposed undertone in the table — this is the warmth you will see in the hair.
  3. Select a toner or color containing the matching neutralizer pigment shown alongside it.
  4. Apply with a low-volume developer (typically 5–10 Vol) for a short processing time, since you are depositing tone rather than lifting further.
Example: hair has lifted to Level 7 and shows yellow-orange/gold-copper warmth. The color wheel calls for a blue-violet ash neutralizer — a toner in the .12 / .21 family — to cancel it.

Level-by-Level Neutralizing Reference

Level Exposed Undertone Neutralizer Family Example Toner
1–2 Red Green Tone .7 (Matte / Green Ash)
3–4 Red-Orange Blue-Green .17 (Blue-Ash Green)
5 Orange-Red Ash-Blue .1 (Ash / Blue)
6 Orange Blue Ash .1 / .11 (Intense Blue Ash)
7 Yellow-Orange (Gold-Copper) Blue-Violet Ash .12 / .21 (Ash Violet / Pearl)
8 Yellow (Gold) Violet / Pearl .2 / .21 (Violet Pearl / Irisé)
9 Pale Yellow Soft Violet / Platinum .02 / .2 (Sheer Violet)
10 Pale Yellow / White Ultra-Pale Silver / Clear Pearl Clear + .2 / 10P

Color Wheel Rules to Remember

  • 1. Green neutralizes Red: Used at the darkest levels of lift (1–3), where the strongest warm/red pigment is exposed.
  • 2. Blue neutralizes Orange: Used at mid-range levels of lift (5–6), as the exposed pigment shifts from red toward orange.
  • 3. Violet neutralizes Yellow: Used at the lightest levels of lift (8–10), where the exposed pigment is yellow or pale yellow.
