Why Lash Mapping Should Follow Eye Shape, Not Just Style

A lash map lays out exactly which length goes at each position along the lash line, creating the overall shape of the set — cat eye, doll eye, squirrel, or natural gradient. But the same mapping style can look very different depending on the client's underlying eye shape.

Keeping inner-corner lengths short (7–8mm) regardless of style protects against eyelid irritation and premature shedding, since the inner corner lashes are typically the shortest and most delicate.

🗺️ How to use this generator

  1. Select the client's eye shape.
  2. Choose the desired mapping style — cat eye, doll eye, squirrel, or natural gradient.
  3. Set the maximum peak length for the longest point in the map.
  4. Follow the generated length-by-length sequence from inner to outer corner.
Example: a cat eye map on almond eyes with a 13mm peak runs 8mm → 9mm → 10mm → 11mm → 12mm → 13mm → 12mm at the outer tip.

Rules to Remember

  • 1. Inner corners stay short no matter the style: Keeping 7–8mm at the inner corner across every mapping style prevents eyelid irritation and reduces premature shedding at the most delicate part of the lash line.
  • 2. Peak length should suit the natural lash length available: A 16mm peak on very short natural lashes risks exceeding a safe extension-to-natural-lash ratio — check natural lash length before committing to a long peak.
