Professional Safety Notice

  • Caution: Always assess natural lash health before every full set or fill — never apply the same fan weight regardless of condition.
  • Caution: Signs of lash damage (thinning, bald patches, breakage) mean reducing fan weight immediately, not continuing the current set plan.

Why Fan Weight Must Match Natural Lash Health

Every natural lash can safely carry a limited amount of extra weight before the follicle is stressed, leading to premature shedding, thinning, or permanent lash loss. Volume fans (3D and above) distribute that weight across more extension strands, but each strand still needs to be thin enough that the total fan weight stays within a safe load.

Matching fan diameter to the client's natural lash condition — not just the desired look — is what keeps mega volume sets from damaging the natural lash underneath.

👁️ How to use this guide

  1. Assess the client's natural lash condition — fine/fragile, healthy normal, or thick/coarse.
  2. Select the desired fan dimension for the look they want.
  3. Use the matching safe extension diameter — never go thicker than what the natural lash and chosen fan dimension can safely support.
Example: a 10D mega volume fan should only ever use ultra-fine 0.03mm extensions, regardless of how healthy the natural lash is, because 10 strands per fan adds up fast.

Rules to Remember

  • 1. Higher fan dimension always means thinner extensions: As fan count goes up, individual extension diameter must go down — this is the only way to keep total fan weight within a safe range.
  • 2. Fragile natural lashes need lighter sets regardless of client preference: If a client wants mega volume but has fine, fragile natural lashes, recommend a lighter fan dimension or longer fill cycle to protect lash health — the look should never override safety.
