Matching DHA Concentration to Skin Type

DHA (dihydroxyacetone) is the active tanning ingredient in spray tan solution, and it reacts differently depending on skin type — very fair skin develops a visible color faster and can appear patchy or orange at high DHA concentrations, while deeper skin tones need a higher DHA percentage to achieve a visible, even result.

The Fitzpatrick scale (a standard skin-type classification used across the beauty and dermatology industry) is the most reliable way to match DHA strength to the client in front of you rather than guessing.

☀️ How to use this guide

  1. Assess the client's Fitzpatrick skin type (very fair through dark/deep).
  2. Select the matching type to see the recommended DHA concentration band.
  3. Advise the client on the correct rinse window for that solution strength.
Example: Type 2 (fair/light) skin typically uses a 9–10% DHA solution with an 8-hour rinse window, or 2–4 hours for a rapid formula.

Rules to Remember

  • 1. Always patch-test for evenness on deeper skin tones: Higher DHA concentrations used on Type 3–4 skin can develop unevenly if the client has dry patches or uneven exfoliation — a patch test catches this before a full-body application.
  • 2. Start sheer on very fair skin: Type 1 skin develops color quickly and is more prone to looking orange or patchy at high DHA — starting with a sheerer solution and building up across sessions gives a more natural result.
