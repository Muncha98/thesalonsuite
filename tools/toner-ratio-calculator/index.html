Professional Safety Notice

  • Caution: Always perform a 48-hour client skin allergy patch test before chemical toning services.
  • Caution: Use only a dedicated toner/gloss developer at the volume your manufacturer specifies — mixing with a standard permanent-color developer can cause unpredictable results.
⚠️ Manufacturer Specification Rule: Ratio math is a universal starting point compatible with major demi-permanent lines including Redken Shades EQ, Wella Color Touch, and Schwarzkopf Igora Vibrance, but exact developer volume and timing vary by line — always cross-reference your own manufacturer's instructions. Mathematical calculations provide proportions, but manufacturer guidelines must always take precedence.

Damp vs Dry Toning: Why the Ratio and Timing Both Matter

Toning damp, freshly lightened hair gives sheer, translucent results because the cuticle is still slightly raised and porous from the lightening service — the toner deposits lightly and blends. Toning dry hair, by contrast, allows for maximum pigment deposit, depth, and longevity, since the cuticle has fully closed.

The developer ratio controls dilution and lift intensity: a 1:1 ratio gives a standard, fuller-strength demi-permanent result, while a 1:2 ratio dilutes the toner for a sheerer, more subtle gloss.

⏱️ How to use this calculator

  1. Measure your toner color amount.
  2. Choose your developer ratio — 1:1 for standard depth, 1:2 for a sheer, diluted gloss.
  3. Mix with 5–10 Volume dedicated toner developer.
  4. Visual-check at 5 and 10 minutes for a standard demi, or every 5 minutes up to 15–20 minutes for a sheer gloss on damp hair.
Example: 30ml of toner at a 1:1 ratio needs 30ml of developer for a 60ml total mix.

Rules to Remember

  • 1. Damp hair tones lighter and sheerer: The still-open cuticle from lightening means less pigment uptake — expect a softer result than the same formula on dry hair.
  • 2. Always visual-check, never process blind: Toner can grab faster than expected on porous or over-processed hair — checking at regular intervals prevents overshooting the target shade.
